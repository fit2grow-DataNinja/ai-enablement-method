# AI Enablement Method

A developing method for assessing and preparing specific AI use cases by bringing together governance, organizational readiness, and use-case requirements.

## Core distinction

- **AI governance** provides frameworks, obligations, responsibilities, and risk-management practices.
- **Organizational readiness** describes the capabilities an organization currently has and may need.
- **Use-case requirements** describe what the specific solution needs, considering its purpose, users, data, autonomy, operating context, and potential impact.

These are connected inputs, but they are assessed separately before their implications are compared.

## Intended method

For a defined use case:

1. Describe the solution, intended purpose, boundaries, users, data, and operating context.
2. Identify relevant governance frameworks, external obligations, and stakeholder expectations.
3. Derive the capabilities and controls required for this use case.
4. Assess current organizational capabilities using evidence and clearly stated assumptions.
5. Identify gaps and define actions, owners, and follow-up.
6. Record a reasoned outcome, such as proceed, proceed with conditions, defer, or stop.

The method is intended to produce a traceable assessment and action plan. The specific criteria, evidence model, and decision rules remain to be developed and validated.

## Status and limits

This is a developing learning and portfolio method. It is not legal advice, a compliance determination, an audit, certification, or proof that a solution is safe or production-ready. Framework versions, applicable law, and project facts must be checked for each real use case.
